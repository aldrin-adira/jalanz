# 01 — Research Findings

Scope: peer-reviewed work published 2020–2029 on (a) why drivers adopt and keep using navigation apps,
(b) how navigation information is presented and acted on, (c) privacy perceptions of location data,
(d) distraction (incl. motorcyclists, important in Malaysia), (e) Malaysian toll context.
Because Malaysia-specific peer-reviewed work on navigation apps is thin, regional (SE/E Asia) studies are used
and their transferability is stated. Recent **non-peer-reviewed market signals** (Oct 2026) are listed
separately and clearly labelled — they reflect the current Malaysian situation but are not research.

## 1. Peer-reviewed evidence (2020s)

### E-01 — Extended TAM for mobile navigation apps (Grade A)
- **Citation:** "Drivers' acceptance of mobile navigation applications: An extended technology acceptance model
  considering drivers' sense of direction, navigation application affinity and distraction perception."
  *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies*, vol. 145, Jan 2021, art. 102507.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581920301099
- **Finding:** Adds sense of direction, app affinity and *distraction perception* to TAM; the model explained
  ~60.5% of variance in intention to use.
- **Design implication:** Perceived distraction directly affects adoption → glanceable UI, voice-first
  guidance, minimal interaction while moving (→ NFR-03, FR-03).

### E-02 — Waze acceptance among Gen Z drivers, Metro Manila (Grade A)
- **Citation:** Gumasing, M.J.J., Bermejo, F.J.C.S., Elpedes, K.T.C., Gonzales, L.F.E., Villajin, A.C.V. (2023).
  "Antecedents of Waze Mobile Application Usage as a Solution for Sustainable Traffic Management among Gen Z."
  *Sustainability* 15(13):10186. https://doi.org/10.3390/su151310186
- **Finding:** Survey of 300 Filipino drivers: system quality, perceived location accuracy, perceived usefulness
  and ease of use drive intention to use; real-time traffic and colour-coded congestion are valued.
- **Transferability:** High — similar SE Asian urban congestion and Waze-heavy usage.
- **Design implication:** Positional accuracy and system quality are adoption drivers, not polish
  (→ NFR-01, NFR-05, FR-04).

### E-03 — Traffic-app information format & route choice, Nanjing (Grade A)
- **Citation:** Qi, X., Ji, Y., Yu, J., Bu, Q., Samal, D.I. (2022). "Influence of smartphone traffic application
  information on drivers' route choice behaviours." *IET Intelligent Transport Systems* 16(10):1413–1426.
  https://doi.org/10.1049/itr2.12218
- **Finding:** RP/SP survey (n=831). When information is delivered and its format (voice or not, 2D vs 3D map)
  affect compliance; aggressive drivers are more time-sensitive; drivers familiar with the network respond more
  to 3D map + voice.
- **Design implication:** Make guidance timing and format configurable; announce early; show time-saving
  explicitly when proposing a reroute (→ FR-03, FR-05).

### E-04 — Factors in navigation-system use incl. trust (Grade A)
- **Citation:** "The factors impacting the use of navigation systems: A study based on the technology acceptance
  model." *Transportation Research Part F*, 2023. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1369847823000074
- **Finding:** Applies TAM and adds **trust** and professional-driver status as factors in attitude and intention.
- **Design implication:** Trust must be earned with transparent ETAs, explained reroutes and visible data
  provenance (e.g., "toll rates as of …") (→ FR-06, NFR-06, NFR-07).

### E-05 — Malaysian drivers' acceptance of in-vehicle applications (Grade A, Malaysia)
- **Citation:** "Modeling User Acceptance of In-Vehicle Applications for Safer Road Environment."
  *Future Internet* 14(5):148, 2022. https://doi.org/10.3390/fi14050148
- **Finding:** Extended TAM with personal/system characteristics, social influence and trust for **Malaysian**
  drivers. Knowledge of the applications significantly affects trust and social influence; Malaysian drivers
  were described as keener to trust their instincts than let technology drive decisions.
- **Design implication:** Explain *why* a route/reroute is suggested; onboarding that builds knowledge of features
  increases trust (→ FR-05, FR-16).

### E-06 — Ride-hailing driver adoption in Malaysia (Grade A, Malaysia)
- **Citation:** "Disentangling Determinants of Ride-Hailing Services among Malaysian Drivers."
  *Information* 13(12):584, 2022. https://doi.org/10.3390/info13120584
- **Finding:** Perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness and **governmental regulations** significantly predict
  intention to use; trust is important.
- **Transferability:** E-hailing drivers are among the heaviest navigation users in Malaysia (news reports note
  Grab drivers rely on Waze — E-M1).
- **Design implication:** Regulatory alignment (PDPA, government guidance) is a positive adoption factor;
  a professional-driver mode is worthwhile (→ NFR-02, FR-13 variants).

### E-07 — Continuance intention for a navigation app (Grade A)
- **Citation:** Suhud, U., Allan, M., et al. (2021). "Customers' Continuance Intention in Using a Mobile
  Navigation App in the Tourism Context: What Factors Will Lead?" *Academica Turistica* 14(2):137–148.
  https://ideas.repec.org/a/prp/jattij/v14y2021i2p137-148.html
- **Finding:** Satisfaction drives continuance; habit affects both satisfaction and continuance.
- **Design implication:** Habit is a moat for incumbents → make switching cheap: import favourites, accept
  shared location links, fast first route (→ FR-01, FR-17).

### E-08 — Systematic review of mobile app usability (Grade A)
- **Citation:** Huang, Z., Benyoucef, M. (2022/2023). "A systematic literature review of mobile application
  usability: addressing the design perspective." *Universal Access in the Information Society* 22(3):715–735.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s10209-022-00903-w
- **Finding:** Maps usability principles → attributes → concrete design features, incl. category-specific features.
- **Design implication:** Use as the usability checklist for UI reviews (→ NFR-08).

### E-09 — Usability of mobile navigation for older adults (Grade A/B)
- **Citation:** Gong, X., Wang, Z., Gong, Q., Xie, H. (2022). "Usability Evaluation of Mobile Navigation for the
  Elderly." https://eurekamag.com/research/105/774/105774931.php
- **Finding:** Older users struggle with complex operation and learnability; proposes an evaluation system
  (effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction; learnability, ease of use, memorability…).
- **Design implication:** Provide a simple mode with large targets and fewer steps (→ FR-14).

### E-10 — Privacy awareness and Google Maps (Grade A)
- **Citation:** Raibulet, C., Wang, K. (2025). "Awareness of privacy and data collection: exploring privacy policy
  effectiveness in Google Maps." *Frontiers in Computer Science* 7. https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1568179
- **Finding:** Navigation apps collect whereabouts, interests and habits; privacy policies are an ineffective
  way of informing users; the study proposes alternatives to raise awareness.
- **Design implication:** In-context, plain-language privacy explanations (not just a policy page); data
  dashboard; opt-in for probe sharing (→ NFR-02, FR-18).

### E-11 — Risky riding incl. phone use, Malaysian motorcyclists (Grade A, Malaysia)
- **Citation:** "Risky riding behaviours among motorcyclists in Malaysia: A roadside survey."
  *Transportation Research Part F*, 2020 (cited elsewhere as Rusli et al., 2020).
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1369847820305192
- **Finding:** Observational study of helmet non-use, red-light running, mobile phone use and turn-signal neglect.
  A later review reports this study observed 0.2% of 72,377 motorcyclists using a phone at signalised
  intersections.
- **Design implication:** Motorcyclists are a large Malaysian user group; a voice-first rider mode is a safety
  feature (→ FR-13).

### E-12 — Distraction & maladaptive phone use: systematic review (Grade A)
- **Citation:** "Is distraction on the road associated with maladaptive mobile phone use? A systematic review."
  *Accident Analysis & Prevention*, 2022. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001457522003359
- **Finding:** 30 of 33 studies found road users with high maladaptive-phone-use scores more likely to use phones
  on the road.
- **Design implication:** Lock text entry while moving; offer voice search (→ NFR-03).

### E-13 — Distraction among motorcyclists, Greater Jakarta (Grade A)
- **Citation:** "From motorcycle taxi to private bikes: how distraction and riding behavior influence traffic
  incidents in Greater Jakarta." *Frontiers in Future Transportation*, 2025.
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2025.1721997/full
- **Finding:** Map navigation, music and adjusting devices are reported as highly distracting because they divide
  visual and cognitive attention — not only phone calls.
- **Transferability:** High (similar motorcycle-taxi and delivery-rider culture).
- **Design implication:** Rider mode with audio/haptic cues and a minimal map (→ FR-13).

### E-14 — Delivery riders' phone & navigation-app use, Vietnam (Grade A)
- **Citation:** "Riding with distraction: Exploring the intention and behaviour of smartphone use while riding
  among motorcyclists in Vietnam." *Accident Analysis & Prevention*, 2025.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001457525000788
- **Finding:** Time pressure raises intention to use a phone while riding; perceived behavioural control is the
  strongest predictor; the study models navigation-app use by delivery riders separately.
- **Design implication:** Pre-trip planning (set route before moving) and multi-stop support for riders (→ FR-13, FR-15).

### E-15 — Toll payment preferences on the North-South Expressway (Grade B, Malaysia)
- **Citation:** "Unveiling Malaysians' Future Payment Preferences by Toll Users at Toll Plazas."
  *International Journal of Research and Innovation in Social Science* VIII(XII), 2024 (publ. Jan 2025).
  https://dx.doi.org/10.47772/IJRISS.2024.8120229
- **Finding:** Using PLUS transaction data, Touch 'n Go is the preferred method, with projected growth for MyRFID
  and bank-card payments.
- **Design implication:** Show payment-method hints per plaza (RFID/TNG/card) in route details (→ FR-06 "Could").

### E-16 — Public-transport TAM with trust in government, Malaysia (Grade A, Malaysia)
- **Citation:** "Adoption of public transport among Malaysian university students: extending the Technology
  Acceptance Model with trust in government." Springer, 2026. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44285-026-00065-8
- **Finding:** Perceived usefulness and ease of use were the only significant predictors; trust in government was
  **not** significant.
- **Design implication:** Government endorsement alone will not drive adoption — usefulness and usability must
  (relevant given the 2026 push for local apps, E-M2) (→ product strategy).

### E-17 — Waze vs Google Maps intention to use, Malaysia (Grade B, Malaysia)
- **Citation:** "Driver's Intention to Use Mobile Navigation Technology: Comparative Study between Waze and Google
  Maps for Travel Route in Malaysia." ICCSIT 2020, Kuala Lumpur (conference, Feb 2020).
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340730716
- **Finding:** n=193; information-system quality and sociability studied; satisfaction moderately and positively
  related to intention; intention to use Google Maps higher than Waze in that sample.
- **Design implication:** Information quality (accurate data, complete POIs) beats social features for Malaysian
  users → prioritise search/data quality over gamification (→ FR-01, FR-02).

### Preprint (Grade C, use with care)
- **E-C1:** "Driving with Guidance: Exploring the Trade-Off Between GPS Utility and Privacy Concerns Among Drivers"
  (Kuwait University), arXiv 2309.12601. Survey + interviews on GPS usage and privacy feelings.
  Implication: supports offering privacy controls without degrading core utility (→ NFR-02).

### Out of decade (background only, do not cite as current)
- Klang Valley stated-preference study on user requirements for traffic apps (~2015), ResearchGate 284012457.
- Klang Valley willingness-to-pay for congestion relief, *Asian Social Science* 11(25), 2015 (mean WTP ≈ RM1.95).

## 3. Synthesis — what Malaysian drivers need (evidence → requirement)

| Theme | Evidence | Resulting requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Usefulness & accuracy dominate adoption | E-02, E-16, E-17, E-M3 | FR-01 search quality, FR-04 live traffic, NFR-01 accuracy |
| Distraction is both a safety and adoption issue | E-01, E-11–E-14 | FR-03 voice-first, FR-13 rider mode, NFR-03 interaction limits |
| Trust needs transparency | E-04, E-05, E-10, E-M3 | FR-05 explainable reroutes, FR-06 toll provenance, NFR-02/07 privacy & disclosure |
| Information format matters | E-03 | FR-03 configurable timing/format |
| Switching costs from habit | E-07 | FR-17 import & share-in, no-login (FR-09) |
| Toll cost is decision-relevant and missing elsewhere | E-15, E-M2, E-M5, E-M6 | FR-05, FR-06, whole of 04-toll-estimator |
| Government push ≠ adoption | E-16, E-M2 | Compete on quality; don't rely on endorsement |
| Older and novice users | E-09 | FR-14 simple mode |

## 4. Research gaps (worth a study in your project)

1. No 2020s peer-reviewed study found that directly elicits **Malaysian** drivers' navigation-app requirements
   (most Malaysian work is pre-2020 or about adjacent technologies). A short survey (n≥150, Klang Valley + one
   non-urban state, BM/EN) using TAM + trust + privacy constructs would fill this.
2. No study found on how Malaysian drivers trade off toll cost against time savings in 2020s conditions.
3. Motorcycle-specific navigation needs in Malaysia are under-studied despite high motorcycle use.
